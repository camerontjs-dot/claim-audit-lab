Architecture

CAL's central design commitment is narrow and load-bearing: a language model contributes evidence, and deterministic code makes the decision. Everything below follows from refusing to let those two roles merge.

Why the verdict cannot come from the model

A neural entailment model produces a distribution over three labels. It is well suited to the question does this passage entail this sentence? and badly suited to the question what should a reviewer be told?, because the second question involves commitments the model has no way to hold: consistency across runs, a stable meaning for each verdict, an audit trail, and behaviour that does not change when a dependency is upgraded.

So the model's output is treated as a signal, never a verdict. The signal enters a rule layer that is versioned, frozen, and SHA-pinned. That layer decides. The practical consequence: you can read the rule that produced any verdict, and re-running the same inputs under the same config reproduces it byte for byte.

●

This bounds the model; it does not make the model correct. Determinism guarantees the same answer every time, including the same wrong answer. What the rule layer buys is that errors are inspectable and fixable at a specific line, not that they are absent. The open defect register lists the ones currently known.

The pipeline

1

Retrieve

bi-encoder · probabilistic

The claim and every supplied passage are embedded; passages are ranked by cosine similarity. Anything below retrieval_floor is discarded before it can influence anything downstream, and at most top_k survive.

retrieval_floor 0.40 top_k 5 decides which evidence is in the room
↓
2

Entail

DeBERTa-v3 NLI · probabilistic

Each admitted passage is scored as premise against the claim as hypothesis, giving entail / neutral / contradict with probabilities. An aggregator selects the contributing passage and produces one support signal.

supported_threshold 0.70 contradicted_threshold 0.70 emits a signal, never a verdict
↓
3

Rule engine

frozen · deterministic

Rules combine the support signal with structural features of the claim: negation, quantities, compound structure, modal strength, scope, sentence type. This layer emits the verdict. Every rule that fires is recorded by id in the trace.

rules_version cal-rules-v1.13.0 pinned by rules_file_sha

The rule families

Rule ids are stable and appear verbatim in every trace, so a verdict can always be traced to the rule that produced it.

FamilyRule idsConcern
A: gating & adverse A1_scope, A2_retrieval_empty, A3_negation_backstop, A3_bound_instantiation_suppressed, A3_conjunct_negation_suppressed, A4_hard_contradiction, A4_negation_consistency Is this claim in scope at all; did any evidence arrive; is the evidence actually against the claim rather than merely silent
B: degree B5_degree Does the entailment signal clear the supported threshold
C: overreach C6a_numeric, C6b_strength_scope, C6c_inferred, C6d_source_scope, C6f_false_caution Is the claim stronger, broader, more numerically precise, or more causal than the passage licenses
P: eligibility P1_eligibility_suppressed, P2_absence_mirror_suppressed Is this source trusted enough to solo-drive a terminal adverse verdict

What "deterministic" actually means here

Four separate guarantees, worth distinguishing because they fail differently:

Frozen thresholds

Every threshold lives in a versioned YAML file, not in code. The loader pins that file's SHA-256 as rules_file_sha and verify_rules_consistency fails loudly if a materialized config ever diverges from it.

Recorded provenance

Each trace carries audit_config_hash and library_version. A verdict from six months ago can be attributed to the exact rule surface that produced it.

Pinned numerics

The inference stack is CPU-only by design. Reproducibility is chosen over throughput, so results do not shift with accelerator availability or non-deterministic kernels.

Non-mutating I/O

Auditing a sealed evidence bundle writes a resealed copy. The input is never modified. With run id and timestamp pinned, outputs are byte-identical.

Structural features, and why they are not the model's job

Several decisions turn on properties of the claim's form rather than its similarity to a passage. Whether a claim is compound (P ∧ Q), whether it carries explicit negation, whether it asserts or hedges, whether it contains a quantity and what kind. These are extracted deterministically (dependency parse, quantity extraction) and passed to the rules alongside the model signal.

Putting them in the rule layer rather than the prompt means they are inspectable, testable in isolation, and fixable without retraining. It is also where the sharpest known defects live, which is the honest cost of the choice: A3 currently has no valid operator over a compound claim, and that produces a false contradicted on human gold. A model would have hidden that failure; the rule layer makes it a specific, addressable line of code.

The layer-separation result

A natural question about this design is whether the split is real or merely tidy, whether layers 1 and 2 are two views of one problem. That was measured, and they are not.

Changing only the retrieval passage unit rescued 2 of 2 retrieval-floor failures and 0 of 10 entailment failures. The intervention was total on one population and inert on the other. Because a claim that never clears the floor is never scored by the entailer, the two populations are disjoint by construction. So theso the size of each problem is unchanged by fixing the other.

What CAL is not

  • Not a fact checker. It has no view on whether a claim is true of the world, only on whether the supplied evidence carries it.
  • Not a retriever over the open web. Evidence is supplied by the caller.
  • Not validated software. An exploratory blind gate cleared in July 2026 on an earlier rule surface; that qualified CAL as a research instrument, not as a validated product. The v1 route is not the shipped default.
  • Not a GxP capability. The vocabulary here is deliberately engineering vocabulary.
